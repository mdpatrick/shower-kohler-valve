Full narration

Adding tabs does not move the foam

You were right. Adding tabs inside the opening does not move the foam or use up its backing margin. Both outside circles stay the same size. Only the gold areas change. My earlier video separately shifted the entire backing plate. That was a hypothetical alignment error, not something that tabs automatically cause.

The water path we care about

Your concern is shower spray getting behind the trim and into the wall. There are two interfaces to address. The factory foam contacts the front of the custom backing. The custom backing also needs an appropriate seal to the travertine. The foam does not have to line up with the opening edge. It needs continuous effective contact around the opening. Keep the factory cover drain open toward the shower.

What the extra margin actually means

Here the purple foam stays fixed. Blue is the backing underneath it. Only in this example, I deliberately position the whole backing two millimeters to one side. A smaller opening leaves more material underneath the same foam. A loss of full width is not a proven leak threshold. We have no validated minimum partial contact width. Keeping the whole band supported is the more forgiving target.

Longer tabs, with room to adjust

This new study extends each tab farther inward, and uses an eighteen by ten millimeter slot. The slot extends toward the center, where the captured rods need more room in our current overlay. These are loose locating features. They help get close, while visual alignment and a suitable retention detail establish the final position. They do not need to be precision dowels.
